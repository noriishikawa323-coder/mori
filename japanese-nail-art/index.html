Japanese nail art is having a moment — and the best part is that most of the designs you’re saving on Pinterest are far easier than they look. Delicate cherry blossoms, tiny flecks of gold leaf, soft milky pinks: none of it requires a salon appointment.

12 Japanese nail designs to try

Start with whichever one matches the length you already have — none of these need extensions.

Cherry blossom

  1. Single blossom accent. One sakura on the ring finger, bare nude on the rest.
  2. Falling petals. Scattered petals drifting from the tip toward the cuticle.
  3. Blossom French tip. Petals along the tip instead of the usual white line.

Gold leaf

  1. Scattered gold flakes. A few irregular pieces over a milky nude base.
  2. Single gold line. One thin vertical stripe down the center of each nail.
  3. Gold tip. Gold foil along the free edge — a French manicure with the volume up.

Traditional patterns

  1. Ichimatsu. Red and white checkerboard, the classic Japanese motif.
  2. Asanoha. The hemp-leaf star pattern, in navy over white.
  3. Seigaiha. Overlapping indigo waves — striking on shorter nails.

Soft and minimal

  1. Milky haze. A sheer white gradient fading from tip to cuticle.
  2. Pressed flower. A real dried blossom sealed under clear gel.
  3. One pearl. A single gold or pearl stud near the cuticle. Nothing else.

Quick tips before you start

  • Paint your base thin. Two thin coats always beat one thick one — thick layers wrinkle under the lamp.
  • Place stickers on a tacky, not-yet-cured base. They grip much better this way.
  • Always cap the free edge. Run your top coat along the tip of the nail to stop chipping.
  • Don’t skip cuticle oil. It’s the difference between “nice nails” and “nice hands.”
