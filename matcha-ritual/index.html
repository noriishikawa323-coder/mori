A $7 matcha latte is mostly milk and labor. Making it at home takes about ninety seconds once you have the right four things — and after that, each cup costs well under a dollar. Here’s the whole kit, with nothing on the list you don’t actually need.

1. Ceremonial Grade Matcha Powder

This is the part that decides whether your matcha tastes sweet and grassy or bitter and flat. Ceremonial grade is first-harvest leaf, stone-ground, and it’s meant to be drunk on its own or in a latte. Culinary grade is for baking — it will taste harsh in a cup.

Don’t buy the cheapest tin you can find. This is the one place on the list where spending a few dollars more genuinely changes the result.

2. Bamboo Whisk Set (Chasen)

A bamboo whisk has around eighty fine prongs, and that’s what breaks up the clumps and builds the layer of fine foam on top. A fork won’t do it. A wire whisk won’t either — the tines are too thick and too far apart.

The set includes the whisk, a bamboo scoop, and a tea spoon. Whisk in a brisk W motion, not a circle.

3. A Matcha Bowl (Chawan)

Wider and shallower than a mug, which is the entire point: the whisk needs room to move sideways. Try it in a tall mug once and you’ll understand immediately — the prongs hit the walls and nothing foams.

It also holds heat well, and it makes the whole thing feel like a small ritual rather than a chore.

4. A Handheld Milk Frother

Only needed if you want lattes, but most people do. Warm the milk, froth it for fifteen seconds, pour it over the whisked matcha. That’s the whole difference between a cafe latte and a sad cup of green milk.
