You don’t need a full kitchen renovation to bring Japanese home cooking into your routine. A single clay pot, the right bowls, and a proper pair of chopsticks change how a meal feels — slower, warmer, more like the table settings you’ve been saving on Pinterest.

Here are the three pieces that make the biggest difference, plus a cookbook if you want recipes to go with them.

1. Donabe Clay Pot

A donabe is the one pot that does it all — rice, hot pot, soup, even steamed vegetables. The clay holds heat evenly and slowly, so food keeps cooking gently after the stove is off, which is part of why donabe rice tastes different from anything made in a metal pot.

A 7-inch size is enough for one or two people and small enough to bring straight to the table.
